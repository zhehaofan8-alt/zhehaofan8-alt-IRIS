The Problem

Main Problem: SANS

SANS (Spaceflight-Associated Neuro-ocular Syndrome): a condition in which weightlessness causes fluid to shift toward an astronaut’s head, putting pressure on the brain + eyes.

Can permanently damage vision
  • Optic nerve swelling
  • Retinal scarring
  • Permanent blind spots
Impairs astronauts’ cognitive + physical ability
  • Cognitive fatigue
  • Brain fog
  • Loss of fine motor skills

Results in significant performance loss and harm during spaceflight, after spaceflight, and spacewalks.

To detect SANS → physical biomarkers + imaging data in the eye and brain structure.

70%of astronauts face swelling in their eyes
15%show more concerning signs of this

Research

NASA Data

What NASA has found about SANS in long-duration crews.

Cause

Microgravity causes fluid shifts toward the head, potentially contributing to SANS

Prevalence

Approximately 70% of long-duration ISS astronauts experience at least one SANS-related ocular finding

Symptoms

Optic disc swelling, retinal folds, changes in eye shape + vision impairment

Health Risks

Potential long-term effects on astronauts' vision + neurological health

Medical Challenges

Limited access to advanced medical equipment + specialists during space missions makes continuous monitoring difficult

The Solution

Solution: Collecting Data

What Will We Collect?

  • Brain: Brain structure + fluid levels + changes in brain volume
  • Eyes: Retinal thickness + optic nerve swelling + changes in eye shape
  • Overall: Whole body in general for versatility

How Will We Collect Data?

  • Smart monitoring bed with MRI capabilities that is lighter than usual
  • The bed regularly scans + collects health measurements when astronauts are on the bed before and during spaceflight
  • MRI data is automatically sent to our health-monitoring website
  • The system continuously updates the astronaut's personalized health profile

The Solution

Solution: Interpreting Data

Interpreted Data

  • ONSD: Optic nerve enlargement
  • Posterior Globe Flattening: Changes in eye shape
  • Refractive Error: Shifts toward farsightedness
  • Brain Structure: Changes in brain volume + fluid + pituitary shape

How The Data Is Interpreted

  • Analyze MRI scans for changes
  • Compare results to personal health baselines
  • Track changes using charts + graphs from before and during spaceflight
  • Use color-coded alerts for unusual results
  • Identify possible early signs of SANS
  • Share findings with astronauts and show next steps

The Solution

Solution for SANS

*All these data can be found on the MRI bed.

SANS Indicators From MRI

Eyes

  • Optic Nerve Sheath Diameter (ONSD): more than 5.0mm to 5.5mm
  • Posterior Globe Flattening: flattening or concavity at the back of the eye
  • Refractive Error: positive shift of +0.50 to +1.00 diopters or more toward farsightedness over a short period

Brain

  • Pituitary Dome Configuration: flattened, concave, or "empty" dome shape on mid-sagittal MRI views
  • Total Intracranial Volume (TIV): reduction in ventricular/sulcal CSF spaces
  • NfL (Neurofilament Light Chain): elevated concentrations in blood/CSF point to ongoing axonal/nerve cell stress + damage
  • Pulse Phase-Lock Loop (PPLL): changes in the amplitude/phase synchronization of these microscopic skull wall vibrations

Health Guidance

Venoconstrictive Leg/Thigh Cuffs

Tight cuffs worn on the legs help trap + regulate blood + fluid movement away from the head

Vitamin & One-Carbon Supplements

Higher levels of B vitamins + folate, alongside antioxidants like coenzyme Q10 (studied by the Italian Space Agency), help protect retinal + neural health

Pressurized Goggles

Specialized eyewear creates an external pressure gradient across the front of the eye to balance intraocular + intracranial pressures

Targeted Diet & Hydration

Regulating sodium + fluid intake minimizes excess volume shifts
